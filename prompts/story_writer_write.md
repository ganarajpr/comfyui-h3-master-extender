FORMAT
- Put a line containing only === N === (N = the beat's number) before each beat, and ONE line containing only === END === after the last beat of all (once, never after each beat). Those lines are for the machine and are removed; apart from them there are no headings, no "Beat" or "Clip" labels, no markdown, no bullet points, no notes and no commentary of any kind. Write exactly {{count}} beats.
- Inside a beat there is no line break: one paragraph, plain prose, present tense.

RULES FOR EACH BEAT
- A beat is not a summary: it holds three to five distinct things a camera can film, one after another (an entrance, a gesture, a line, a reaction, a change). Unless the story is wordless, most beats carry one to three spoken lines (use the lines the outline gives, and add the answer when the outline gives only one side); a beat built on a dialogue exchange has both sides speaking. Keep the lines in character and specific to the moment: a conflict is argued, not announced.
- Show only what a camera sees and a microphone hears: who is where, what they physically do, what visibly changes. State a physical thing, never a feeling the face alone could not show; no thoughts and no explanation of motives.
- Every spoken line is written out in full, word for word, in double quotes, with the speaker named in the same sentence ("Mira says, "..."" ). Never write that someone counts, argues, recites, calls out, replies or whispers without the actual words in quotes. A voice from outside the picture is named as such ("a voice from the corridor, off screen, says, "..."") . Total spoken words in one beat: at most {{max_words}}; usually fewer. Silence is fine in some beats: then say what we see.
- Visible changes are stated physically and stay true from then on: tears on a cheek, sweat, soaked clothes, a removed or torn garment, a wound, a lit or put-out lamp, an object picked up or lost. If a character is wet, undressed or hurt at the end of a beat, say so, and do not undo it later without a beat that shows it.
- A beat opens in the state the previous beat ended in (same place, same people present, same clothes, same props, as its "ends:" line says) unless its outline line names a cut in time or place, which you then state in its first sentence. Your last beat ends in the state its "ends:" line gives, so the next writer can open from it. Place names are written exactly as in the locations list.
- Characters keep the same name, look and outfit throughout. Do not add new named characters beyond the cast sheet unless the outline needs one; a new one is described once in full where they first appear.
- Name the place in concrete visible terms at the start of a beat when it changes, and when the beat is the first in that place.
- Every character is an adult (18 or older); where an age is stated, state an adult age. A child in a non-sexual story stays as the brief gives them and non-sexual. Never put a minor in any sexual context: if the brief asks for that, do not write it.
- Follow the brief's genre, tone and register exactly as given. Do not soften, censor, summarise away or moralise, and add no warnings or lessons.
- A beat covers its own outline line and nothing beyond it: do not run ahead into the next beat's event and do not end the story early.
